Irish airspace

Which governments and private jets flew into Ireland for the European Political Community and EU leaders’ meetings in Dublin in November 2026, from open flight-tracking data.

Ireland hosted the European Political Community on Thursday 12 November 2026 and an informal meeting of EU leaders on Friday 13 November, both at the Convention Centre Dublin, as part of its EU Presidency.1 Most aircraft broadcast their position, altitude and identity over ADS-B, and a network of volunteer receivers records these signals and publishes every day’s flights as open data, which makes it possible to see which government planes and private jets came to Ireland for the summits and when they arrived.

Figure 1. Government and military planes flying in and out of Ireland, with each one labelled by the country that sent it, from 06:00 on Wednesday 11 November to midnight on Friday 13 November. Hollow circles are planes parked at an Irish airport. Times are Irish time. Data: adsb.lol (ODbL).

Government planes

On an ordinary weekday no more than two foreign government or military aircraft land in Ireland, while on the day of the European Political Community there were 17, most of which arrived in a wave between 09:00 and 11:30 that morning, and on the Friday there were 17 again as they came back to collect their leaders and fly home. Business jet numbers stayed within their usual range of about 15 to 27 a day, although several of the jets that look private on paper were in fact government aircraft, such as those of Monaco, Serbia, North Macedonia, Georgia and Croatia.

Several governments set their leader down at Dublin and then flew the empty plane on to Shannon, which suggests that Dublin ran short of parking space. Hungary, Switzerland, Czechia, Slovakia, Bulgaria, Monaco and Serbia all did this. France, Portugal, Italy, Romania, Ukraine and the United States used Casement Aerodrome at Baldonnel, the Air Corps base, instead of Dublin Airport.

The departures on Friday follow the two meetings. The leaders of countries outside the EU, who came only for the European Political Community, left on Friday morning, with Switzerland gone by 06:20 and Azerbaijan, Monaco and Montenegro by 10:00. The planes parked at Shannon flew back to Dublin between late morning and mid-afternoon, and most EU leaders left in a second wave between about 16:40 and 19:00 once their own meeting had finished, among them those of France, Germany, Czechia, Bulgaria, Lithuania, Slovakia, Croatia, Poland and Italy.

Private jets

Most of the private jets that used Irish airports during the week were charter or fractional-ownership aircraft from operators such as NetJets, VistaJet and Flexjet, which carry different customers from one day to the next. Jets registered in the United States are often held by trust companies or holding companies, so the public register names the company and not the person who flies on the plane. About the data

The flight data comes from adsb.lol, which publishes a full day of worldwide flight tracks from volunteer receivers every day under the Open Database Licence. An aircraft counts here when it was on the ground, or below 1,500 feet, within 9 km of an Irish airport. Baldonnel and Weston are only 3 km apart, so they are shown together. Some government aircraft switch off or hide their transponders and do not appear in the data, so these counts are a minimum. Flights by the Irish Air Corps itself are left out. The country of each government plane comes from its registration or callsign, and the aircraft type and the military flag come from the aircraft database that adsb.lol publishes with the tracks.

Code and data. The scripts in analysis/airspace download each day’s archive, pick out the aircraft that used an Irish airport, draw the timelapse and write the tables on this page. The daily counts, the list of aircraft and their tracks are in analysis/airspace/data.
