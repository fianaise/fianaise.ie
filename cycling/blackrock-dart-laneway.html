The Blackrock DART laneway

A walled lane about 1.2 metres wide carries the Coastal Mobility Route past Blackrock DART station. A wider replacement was approved in 2024, but work has not started.
Published

7 November 2026

Part of Cycling infrastructure in Dublin. See also Shankill to Killiney.

The problem

Between Blackrock Park and Bath Place, at the entrance to Blackrock DART station, the Coastal Mobility Route squeezes into a single lane between high walls. It’s the only direct link between the park’s paths and the station, and Google Maps sends cycle routes through it.

The lane is about 1.2 metres wide (Part 8 report), and about 90 metres of it has walls on both sides (IrishCycle, January 2026). Two bikes can’t pass each other in it. Nor can a bike and a buggy, or two buggies. Signs tell people on bikes to dismount, so everyone queues at each end and goes through in turns. Local councillor Marie Baker put it plainly: “you end up flattening yourself against the wall if you come up to a cyclist” (IrishCycle, April 2024).

View on OpenStreetMap

Current status of the laneway

DLR’s Blackrock DART to Park Active Travel Scheme replaces the lane with a new route through part of the garden of Deepwell House next door: 130 metres of two-way cycle track 3 to 3.65 metres wide, a separate 2 metre footpath, grass verges, and a new bridge over the Priory Stream. The old lane will be closed off (DLR Part 8 report; DLR PPN).

Construction

  • Planning. The Part 8 consultation ran from 28 June to 9 August 2024 and drew 321 submissions. Councillors agreed the Chief Executive’s recommendations at their October 2024 meeting (DLR; Citizen Space; Chief Executive’s report).
  • Land. The council’s compulsory purchase order for part of the Deepwell House garden went to An Coimisiún Pleanála (formerly An Bord Pleanála) on 11 November 2025 and closed with no objections received on 8 December 2025 (case 323857; IrishCycle, January 2026). At the time, Cllr Baker said there was “some paperwork to be completed and then detailed design to take place before work commences.”
  • Site investigation. The council closed the lane from 24 to 28 August 2026 for ground investigation. Cllr Conor Dowling said: “While the main works will not commence for a few months, this is real progress” (IrishCycle, August 2026).
  • Construction. As of 7 November 2026 I could find no published start date, construction period or budget. The council’s report treats cost estimates as commercially sensitive.

How many people cycle here, and how many go around?

The council counted people on the laneway route on three weekdays in November 2022, from 7am to 7pm. Two survey points along the route averaged 406 and 476 cyclists a day and 1,373 and 772 pedestrians a day (Part 8 report, Table 4-3; a third point recorded 68 cyclists).

DLR also runs permanent bicycle counters and publishes the hourly counts as open data. Three are on Rock Road at Booterstown, about a kilometre west of the lane: the cycle track, the bus lane, and a path counter it calls “Rock Road Park”. Another is on the Coastal Mobility Route at Seapoint, about a kilometre and a half east. Here’s what they counted over the same three days and hours.

Figure 1. Average cyclists per weekday, 7am to 7pm, 8 to 10 November 2022. Blue: DLR’s permanent counters on Rock Road and at Seapoint. Orange: the council’s survey of the laneway route. Data: DLR, bicycle counts (CC BY), and the Part 8 report. Code: make_cycling_charts.py. Open full screen.

On those days about 1,670 cyclists a day passed the Rock Road counters and about 900 passed Seapoint, but only 400 to 480 used the laneway route. So the lane carried at most about half as many cyclists as the coastal route at Seapoint. Most people cycling the coast between Booterstown and Seapoint appear to stay on Rock Road and go through Blackrock village rather than take the park and the lane.

That’s consistent with people avoiding the lane, but it doesn’t prove it. Some of the people on these counters are heading to Blackrock village or turning inland, and nobody counts which way each person goes. The council’s own counts are also from just three days. What the numbers do give is a baseline. Once the new path opens, the same counters will show whether people switch from the road to the park route.

Cycling on this stretch has held steady since the pandemic peak. In 2025 the Rock Road road counters averaged about 1,130 cyclists a day, the park path about 520, and Seapoint about 1,030, every day of the year.

Questions for the council

  1. When will construction of the Blackrock DART to Park Active Travel Scheme start, and how long will it take?
  2. Has detailed design been completed, and has a contractor been appointed?
  3. What’s the published budget for the scheme?
  4. During construction, what’s the diversion for people walking and cycling between Blackrock Park and the station?
  5. Will the council put a permanent counter on the new path, so it can show how many people switch to it from Rock Road?

The scheme’s contact is DLR’s Capital Projects Office, capitalprojects@dlrcoco.ie.
