Primary School Survey 2026

What parents, school staff and boards of management told the Department of Education about primary school ethos.

Part of Religion in Ireland.

Over 90% of Irish primary schools are denominational, yet recent survey data (June 2026) shows substantial and widespread parental demand for multi-denominational education. The maps and graphs below explore that gap, school by school and county by county, with a particular focus on how the preferences of parents differ from those of school staff and boards of management.

The data comes from the Department of Education and Youth’s 2025–2026 Primary School Survey and its results summary report. The results for staff and boards of management are not in the spreadsheet the Department publishes, only in the summary report. The code is on GitHub. The figures are interactive.

Parents want multi-denominational education more than staff and boards of management

NoteHow the staff and board of management survey was run

The school staff and board of management survey was open to primary school staff and members of boards of management nationally. The target population was 84,285 staff and board members. A total of 32,999 responses were submitted, and 32,901 valid responses were kept after validation. That is a validated response rate of 39.0% of the eligible population. (Source: results summary report, section 4.1, p. 25.)

There is a mismatch between the schools parents want for their children and the schools available to them

Figure 3. Stated ethos preference among households with a child not yet in school (n = 56,227), grouped by the type of school they intend to use. Each bar is 100% of the households intending that school type, split by whether they prefer a multi-denominational (green) or denominational (grey) ethos. This isolates demand for change: households intending a denominational school but preferring a multi-denominational one represent unmet demand, while households intending and preferring the same type represent confirmed preference. Percentages are recomputed from raw counts reported in the source, and the base (56,227) can’t be independently checked against the published survey spreadsheet. Source: Department of Education and Youth, Primary School Survey results summary report (June 2026), section 3.1.2, p. 14. Code: plot_survey_summary.py.
