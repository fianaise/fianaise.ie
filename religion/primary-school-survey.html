Primary School Survey 2026

What parents, school staff and boards of management told the Department of Education about primary school ethos.

Part of Religion in Ireland. See also Schools for who runs Ireland’s primary schools today.

Over 90% of Irish mainstream primary schools are denominational, yet recent survey data (June 2026) shows substantial and widespread parental demand for multi-denominational education. The charts and maps below explore that gap, school by school and county by county, with a particular focus on how the preferences of parents differ from those of school staff and boards of management.

The data comes from the Department of Education and Youth’s 2025–2026 Primary School Survey: its results summary report (PDF, June 2026), its results for all primary schools and its School Planning Area results (spreadsheets, as published on the survey page; the links are to the copies this site uses). The results for staff and boards of management are not in the spreadsheets, only in the summary report. The code is on GitHub. The figures are interactive, and each one has buttons to download its data as a CSV file, save it as an image, or share it.

These findings were presented at the Department’s Annual Education Research Summit on 5 November 2026: see the slides.

NoteHow the numbers are counted
  • National and county shares of parents and of staff and boards (45%, 40.2%, 34.7%, Figures 1, 2, 5 and 6) are the Department’s own, from the summary report. They cover all primary schools, mainstream and special. They match the School Planning Area results, which also include special schools, and schools whose own results were held back because too few parents answered: the planning-area spreadsheet gives 90,177 of 200,365 households (45.0%) preferring a multi-denominational school. Special schools are a small part of these totals: the 119 special schools with a published result account for 2,010 parent responses, about 1% of the total.
  • Counts of schools (Figure 3, Figure 4 and Table 1) come from the results for each school, because only they say which school is which. They cover mainstream primary schools only (3,067 in the survey). The 150 special schools are left out: the Department’s school lists, which give each school’s ethos and location, cover mainstream schools only, and the survey file labels many special schools run by the HSE or disability charities as “Denominational”. The shares of pupils and schools that are multi-denominational today (Figure 2) are also for mainstream schools. 180 denominational schools had too few responses for their result to be published.
  • “Most parents want a multi-denominational ethos” has two definitions on this page. Current parents: more than 50% of the parents of children in the school who answered prefer a multi-denominational ethos. This is the result each school received, and it gives 485 schools. Current and preschool parents: 50% or more of all parents linked to the school, adding parents of children not yet in school who named it, prefer a multi-denominational ethos. This gives 573 schools. 471 schools meet both.

The first survey of its kind

The Department described the survey as “the largest of its kind ever undertaken in Ireland” (press release, 4 November 2025).

What the Department set out to do

“Assess parental demand for different types of primary school provision”: ethos, co-education or single-sex, and English- or Irish-medium.

Who was asked

  • Parents of children in primary school, and of children not yet enrolled
  • One response per household, validated by Eircode
  • Separately, school staff and boards of management

NoteHow the staff and board of management survey was run

The school staff and board of management survey was open to primary school staff and members of boards of management nationally. The target population was 84,285 staff and board members. A total of 32,999 responses were submitted, and 32,901 valid responses were kept after validation. That is a validated response rate of 39.0% of the eligible population. (Source: results summary report, section 4.1, p. 25.)

There is a mismatch between the schools parents want for their children and the schools available to them

Figure 6. Stated ethos preference among households with a child not yet in school (n = 56,227), grouped by the type of school they intend to use. Each bar is 100% of the households intending that school type, split by whether they prefer a multi-denominational (green) or denominational (grey) ethos. This isolates demand for change: households intending a denominational school but preferring a multi-denominational one represent unmet demand, while households intending and preferring the same type represent confirmed preference. Percentages are recomputed from raw counts reported in the source, and the base (56,227) can’t be independently checked against the published survey spreadsheet. Source: Department of Education and Youth, Primary School Survey results summary report (June 2026), section 3.1.2, p. 14. Schools covered: all primary schools, mainstream and special, as the Department counted them. Code: make_charts.py.
